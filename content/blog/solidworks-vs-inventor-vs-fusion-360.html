"Which CAD software should I learn?" is one of the questions I hear most in my training sessions. I use SolidWorks, Autodesk Inventor and Fusion 360 for client work, and the honest answer is that all three are excellent. The right one depends on what you want to do. Here's how they compare.

SolidWorks

SolidWorks, from Dassault Systèmes, is one of the most widely used mechanical CAD tools in industry. It's a Windows desktop application known for an approachable interface and strong part, assembly and drawing tools, with simulation available inside the same environment.

A big advantage for job seekers is its certification path: CSWA (Associate), CSWP (Professional) and CSWE (Expert). A certification on your CV is clear proof of your skills.

Best for: people aiming for product design and manufacturing roles, especially where job adverts ask for SolidWorks.

Autodesk Inventor

Inventor is Autodesk's professional mechanical design tool, also a Windows desktop application. Because it comes from the makers of AutoCAD, it works very smoothly with DWG drawings, which is a big plus in workplaces with years of AutoCAD files.

It has powerful tools for sheet metal, frame structures and large assemblies, and is commonly bought as part of Autodesk's Product Design & Manufacturing Collection alongside AutoCAD.

Best for: machine design, sheet-metal work and companies already using Autodesk products.

Fusion 360

Fusion 360 is Autodesk's cloud-connected platform. It runs on both Windows and macOS and combines CAD, CAM (toolpaths for CNC machines), simulation, rendering and electronics design in one product. Files live in the cloud, which makes sharing and collaborating easy.

Autodesk offers free licences for students and educators and a free option for personal, non-commercial use, though the terms change from time to time, so check Autodesk's website for the current details.

Best for: startups, makers, CNC and 3D-printing workflows, Mac users and anyone who wants an all-in-one tool.

So which one should you learn?

  • Looking for a job? Search local job adverts and see which tool comes up most for the roles you want. That's the most reliable guide.
  • Still a student? All three offer student licences, often through your university, so try them and see which one clicks.
  • Building your own products? Fusion 360's built-in CAM and cloud sharing are hard to beat for a small team.

The skills transfer

Here's the good news: the core ideas of sketches, constraints, features, assemblies and drawings are the same in all three. Once you're confident in one, learning another takes weeks, not years. Focus on solid fundamentals and good design habits rather than worrying about the "perfect" software.
