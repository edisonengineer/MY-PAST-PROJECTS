Most people who feel "slow" in AutoCAD aren't missing skills. They're losing seconds on every single command, hunting through ribbons and menus. Those seconds add up to hours every week. Here are ten habits I teach in my free weekly webinar that make the biggest difference.

1. Type command aliases instead of clicking icons

Almost every command has a short alias. Type it on the command line and press Enter or Space: L for Line, PL for Polyline, C for Circle, O for Offset, TR for Trim, EX for Extend and F for Fillet. Learn the ten you use most first. Your left hand stays on the keyboard and your right hand stays on the mouse.

2. Press Space (or Enter) to repeat the last command

After finishing a command, pressing Space or Enter on an empty command line starts it again. Drawing ten circles? Type C once, then just press Space before each new one.

3. Use Space instead of Enter

In most situations, Space works exactly like Enter (except when typing text). It's bigger and right under your thumb, which makes the alias-and-confirm rhythm much faster.

4. Open the Properties palette with Ctrl+1

Ctrl+1 toggles the Properties palette. Select any object to change its layer, colour, linetype or dimensions directly, without hunting for the right command.

5. Copy formatting with Match Properties (MA)

Type MA, pick a source object, then pick the objects you want to match. Layer, colour, linetype and more are copied across in one go. It's perfect for tidying up drawings you've received from someone else.

6. Shift + right-click for a one-off object snap

Don't want to turn on every object snap permanently? Hold Shift and right-click to open the snap menu, then choose Midpoint, Center, Perpendicular or whatever you need for just that one point.

7. Toggle drawing aids with the function keys

F3 toggles object snap, F8 toggles Ortho (perfectly horizontal and vertical lines), F10 toggles Polar tracking and F12 toggles Dynamic Input. Switching them on and off from the keyboard keeps you in the flow.

8. Copy with a base point: Ctrl+Shift+C

Regular copy-paste places objects relative to an awkward corner. Ctrl+Shift+C lets you pick the exact base point first, so pasting into another drawing lands objects precisely where you want them.

9. Double-click the mouse wheel to zoom extents

Lost somewhere in a huge drawing? Double-click the middle mouse button to zoom to everything. Scroll to zoom and hold the wheel to pan, so you'll rarely need the zoom commands at all.

10. Make your own aliases

If you use a command constantly and its alias is awkward, change it. On the Manage tab, open Edit Aliases in the Customization panel to edit the alias file (acad.pgp). Restart AutoCAD, or type REINIT, to load your changes.

Practice makes it automatic

Pick three shortcuts from this list and force yourself to use them for a week. Once they're automatic, add three more. Within a month you'll be drafting noticeably faster.
