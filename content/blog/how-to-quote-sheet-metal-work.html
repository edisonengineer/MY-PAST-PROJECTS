Sheet-metal jobs look simple: a few brackets, a cover, a channel. But the price hides in details that are easy to skip: the real size of the flat blank, how much of the sheet is wasted, how many metres the laser cuts and how many times the press brake bends. Here is a method that gets the price right every time.

1. Work out the flat blank, not the finished size

A bracket with 50 mm and 30 mm outside flanges is not cut from an 80 mm strip. When the metal bends, the outside stretches and the inside squeezes, so the flat blank is a little shorter. For 1.5 mm steel with a 1.5 mm inside radius and a K-factor of 0.33, each 90° bend takes away about 2.9 mm, so the blank is 77.1 mm. That matters for cutting, for weight, and for whether the part fits on the sheet. (My free bend allowance calculator explains the formula.)

2. Decide how you charge for material

  • By weight (parts' weight × price per kg + 10–20% scrap) is fair for small parts and small batches, where the rest of the sheet goes back on the rack.
  • By whole sheets is right when the job uses most of a sheet or the offcut is useless. Work out how many blanks fit on a 1220 × 2440 sheet, both ways round, with about 10 mm round the edge and 5 mm between parts.

3. Cutting is priced by the metre

Laser and plasma shops charge by cut length plus a small charge for each pierce (each hole or start point). The cut length is the outline of the blank plus every hole and cut-out. A 77 × 120 mm blank with four holes is about 0.53 m of cutting and five pierces.

4. Bending is priced per bend

Each bend is a press-brake stroke, so count bends × parts. Add a set-up charge for the job: setting the back gauge and tools takes the same time for 1 part or 100.

5. Finishing by area

Powder coating and painting are priced per m². Use the flat blank area (the bends don't change it much) and remember both sides.
